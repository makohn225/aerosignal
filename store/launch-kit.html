Description (4,000):

Most accidents have happened before, in a similar aircraft, on a similar route, in similar weather. AeroSignal reads your planned flight against public accident history and tells you, in plain language, what has hurt pilots in flights like yours and what to do about it.

BEFORE YOU GO

  • A GO, CAUTION or NO GO verdict with the reasons, built from weather, TFRs, NOTAMs, diversion airports, your currency and your personal minimums.
  • Accident lessons matched to your route, aircraft, phase of flight and conditions, with the NTSB reports behind them.
  • Weight, balance and fuel checked against your POH numbers and the FAA reserve rules.
  • A kneeboard card with runways, winds, weather, a clearance pad and a passenger briefing.
  • Alerts before departure when the weather, TFRs or airport closures change.

PLAN AND FLY

  • FAA sectional, terminal area and IFR enroute charts, saved on your device for flying without signal.
  • Airway routing and a nav log with winds, headings, times and fuel.
  • Approach plates on the map and Follow my aircraft with live GPS.
  • An approach profile that uses the FAA published glidepath for the runway.
  • Spoken callouts for traffic, TFRs ahead and the glidepath, so your eyes stay outside.
  • Measure distances and landing zones with two fingers, range rings and position reports.

AFTER YOU LAND

  • Approach debrief: your flown approach against the published glidepath, with stabilized approach gates at 1,000 and 500 feet.
  • One tap logbook entries and currency tracking.

AeroSignal was built by a retired Marine Corps aviator, after years of seeing the same kinds of mishaps repeat because no one had time to read every report before a flight. It uses public NTSB and FAA data, keeps your logbook and flights on your device, and never sells your data or shows advertising.

Not for navigation. AeroSignal is a planning and safety awareness aid and does not replace official charts, briefings or your aircraft manual. The pilot in command is responsible for every flight. Never enter CUI or classified information.

Apple App Privacy answers (privacy "nutrition label")

Based on the current privacy policy. Tracking: No (AeroSignal does not track users across other companies' apps or websites).

  • Contact Info, Email Address: collected, linked to the user, used for App Functionality (account sign in). Not used for tracking.
  • Contact Info, Name: collected only if entered in the optional profile, linked to the user, App Functionality.
  • User Content, Customer Support: feedback messages, linked to the user when signed in, used for App Functionality.
  • User Content, Other User Content: flights, aircraft, checklists and logbook, only when account sync is turned on; planned routes and times for flight alerts, deleted after the flight. Linked to the user, App Functionality.
  • Identifiers, Device ID: the push notification address for flight alerts, linked to the user, App Functionality.
  • Usage Data, Product Interaction: page views counted by Vercel Web Analytics, not linked to the user, Analytics.
  • Diagnostics, Other Diagnostic Data: browser type and app version sent with feedback, linked when signed in, App Functionality.
  • Location: not collected. GPS position stays on the device; lookups of nearby airports send coordinates that are not stored or tied to the user.

Confirm before submitting: the plain language plan reader can send plan text you typed to an outside AI service to be rewritten. That text is not stored by AeroSignal. Apple treats data sent to a third party as collected only if it is kept, so this is normally not declared, but mention it in the review notes if asked.

Text that needs your approval before launch

Proposed sentence for the privacy page: "In the phone and tablet apps, your location is used on the device to show your aircraft and record your track, and the local network is used only to receive data from a portable ADS-B receiver. Launch steps

  1. Form the LLC and get written approval for the outside activity.
  2. Create the Apple Developer and Google Play accounts in the company name.
  3. Build the apps (native folder in the repository) and test them on a phone.
  4. Approve the privacy sentence above.
  5. Create the listings with this text, upload the screenshots and icons, and submit for review.
